# Family reference audit

Superseded by the [claim-level recheck](family-reference-recheck.md). The references below
were real, but several mappings were too broad; version 1.2 uses direct practical sections.

Audited 2026-10-18. Scope: all six stories and all 22 decision steps under
`content/family/`, which render the "sources of ideas and information" report.
This is a reference audit, not clinical/cultural approval or an audit of the
separate clinical courses. Stories remain Draft with their Content TODOs.

## Verdict and corrections

The original references are real and substantively related. The weakness was
some overly broad step-to-source mappings, not fabricated publications.

- Added WHO-SHARED-CARE for division of domestic/care work; nutrition guidance
  alone does not substantiate assignment of cooking/cleaning tasks.
- Added WHO-SUPPORT to the two steps that cited only a procedure classification
  while practising listening or seeking advice. Classification cannot establish
  a communication technique's effectiveness.
- Removed LACTMED-GINGER from the unspecified mixed-herbal-product story. The
  monograph is real, but this dialogue never identifies ginger. The directly
  relevant supplements chapter already supports the ingredient/product distinction.
- Added a page locator and procedure codes to the two DTAM cultural references.
- Version 1.1 applies to newly created rooms. Existing rooms retain their pinned
  story/reference snapshots. No dialogue, choice, skill or safety rule was changed.

All seven URLs were opened through the web research tool. DTAM PDF text was
reopened at the relevant page after an initial extraction failure; a separate
terminal download returned HTML, so no local PDF rendering is claimed. The WHO
full-guideline download and some NCBI HTML pages were blocked/timed out; their
search snippets were not substituted for direct reading. The usable WHO article
and NIH supplements PDF above were read directly.

## Limits and editorial follow-up

The report's evidence categories describe a cited document's role, not a blanket
evidence grade for Thai traditional medicine. No new efficacy/safety claim,
clinical procedure or contraindication was authored. Experts still need to
review cultural wording and topic-specific evidence before approval. Source IDs
provide traceability; they do not establish educational effectiveness, validate
the four-domain report, or turn the authored dialogue into a guideline quotation.

The schema currently attaches sources to a step, not to individual feedback
sentences. This audit records that distinction without changing the schema.
